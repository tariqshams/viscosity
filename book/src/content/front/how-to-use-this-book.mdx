title: How to use this book
order: 2
---

**Fifteen minutes, four times a day, for fifty days.** Every chapter is
sized to about fifteen minutes, including its exercises and quiz. Four
chapters make a day. The [50-day plan](/book/days/) shows where you are.

**Start each day with your reviews.** As you read, you’ll meet *Quick
recall* boxes. Try to answer each one in your head *before* you reveal it,
then say honestly whether you remembered. That one tap adds the card to
your personal deck. The [Review](/book/review/) page then brings each card back
just before you’d forget it: first after a few days, then weeks, then
months. A few minutes a day is enough, and it is the difference between
having read this book and *knowing* it.

<Callout kind="intuition" label="Why recall works">
Reading something again feels productive but fades fast. Pulling an answer
out of your own memory is harder, and the effort is exactly what makes it
stick. In a classic experiment, students who practised recalling a passage
remembered more of it a week later than students who spent the same time
rereading it.
</Callout>

**Step through the diagrams.** Diagrams marked *Step through* move only when
you tap **Next**. Go at your own pace, and go back whenever something
doesn’t click. The colours mean the same thing everywhere: if a field is
orange in one picture, it is orange in all of them.

**Type the code.** Every *Try it* block is editable and runnable. On a
phone, a row of symbol keys appears above the keyboard while you edit, and
⤢ opens the editor full screen. Your edits are saved in this browser.

**Do the exercises before the solutions.** Each exercise has tests, hints
that reveal one at a time, and a worked solution that also shows the
common wrong turns. Struggling for a few minutes first is not wasted time.
That struggle is where the learning happens.

**Your data stays with you.** Your progress, review deck and code edits
live only in this browser’s storage. The Review page can export a backup
and import it on another device.
